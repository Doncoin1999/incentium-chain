Subnet pools

Every subnet has its own token, Alpha, issued like ICT. Staking on a subnet means buying its Alpha in a pool that nobody owns, and Alpha pays its holders in ICT. This page lists every pool, read straight from the chain as you watch, and lets you stake from your own wallet.

How the pools work

  • Issued like ICT. Subnet 1 has Alpha1, Subnet 2 Alpha2, and so on. None exists when a subnet opens; then exactly 7,200 are issued a day, halving on ICT's curve from the subnet's own first day, up to 21,000,000. No owner or DAO can change this.
  • Half is a bonus for the work. 3,600 a day go to the subnet's miners, validators and owner, 50/38/12, on top of their pay in ICT. The owner's part is released over 6 months.
  • Half is stock. It waits outside the pool and is released into it block by block. While the price is more than 20 % below its 30-day average, the stock is held back and released later.
  • Alpha pays ICT. The validators' 38 % of the subnet's ICT goes to everyone with Alpha staked with them; each validator keeps a take of 0–18 % that it chooses, and lowers or raises (at most once a month). Nothing buys Alpha back.
  • Staking is buying. Staking ICT on a subnet puts it in the pool and gives you Alpha, staked with a validator you choose. Unstaking sells the Alpha back. The subnet's share of issuance follows the ICT in its pool.
  • Opening. For the first 10 days nothing can be traded: the stock collects and half of the auction price waits for the pool. Then the pool opens at a start price worked out on chain from the ICT an Alpha is expected to pay (20 % required return), with real ICT and Alpha only.
  • Nobody owns it. The pool's account has no key: only trades move what is in it. 0.5 % per trade, half burned and half left in the pool.
  • When a subnet ends. No more Alpha is issued, waiting stock is burned, and the pool stays open for unstaking.

Staking on a subnet carries price risk. If you stake 1,000 ICT you get the dividends plus what your Alpha is worth when you unstake, not necessarily 1,000 ICT. With 7,200 new Alpha a day the price of each Alpha tends to fall while the dividends make up for it; many stakers leaving at once push it down hard.
